# Recorded worker wake order around CoreCLR GC restart

The offline replay retained all 44 Callsite and scheduler-switch associations spanning 30 distinct exact callsite invocations in the same Roslyn run. Forty-one associations had raw state 8193 and the ordered native out, waking, wakeup and switch-in sequence, with no visible recorded-order mask. Six are the previously verified linked blocking-GC cases. Twenty are disjoint from every linked blocking-GC outer envelope, 15 have linked overlap without the strict nesting, and three have a different switch-out state or receipt order. | Recorded timestamp difference in the six strict cases | Minimum | Median | Maximum |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Switch-out to `waking` | 5.935 ms | 11.806 ms | 18.273 ms |
| `waking` to `wakeup` | 15.101 µs | 15.926 µs | 25.740 µs |
| `wakeup` to switch-in | 0.858 µs | 2.622 µs | 5.794 µs |

For all six cases, `waking` is 73.883–150.566 µs after the mapped `RestartEEStart` bounds and 47.826–180.573 µs before the mapped `RestartEEStop` bounds. Switch-in precedes the stop bounds by 30.900–149.039 µs, and the exact GC wait callsite return follows them by 78.514–1,941.534 µs. The `waking` publication CPU differs from the later `wakeup` publication CPU in all six. The latter CPU matches the CPU of the switch-in record in all six. These are recorded callback relationships. They do not expose the wake-list branch or an IPI. [Kernel wake path](https://github.com/microsoft/WSL2-Linux-Kernel/blob/a989171929bed4ea681be8d77333845ce1c1732a/kernel/sched/core.c#L4175-L4208) · [Kernel wakeup and switch callsites](https://github.com/microsoft/WSL2-Linux-Kernel/blob/a989171929bed4ea681be8d77333845ce1c1732a/kernel/sched/core.c#L6938-L6944)

The pinned CoreCLR source brackets `ResumeAllThreads` with restart events. That function invokes `SetWaitForGCEvent`, and the implementation of `WaitUntilGCComplete(false)` can wait on that event. The observed order is compatible with this release path. The capture supplies no exact setter time or waker identity for an individual receipt. The kernel emits the switch callback before the context switch, so the recorded timestamps cannot supply exact physical scheduler or GC components. [CoreCLR restart](https://github.com/dotnet/dotnet/blob/5254d8bcf59917edc1e1abc33bdf2442b77b1c70/src/runtime/src/coreclr/vm/threadsuspend.cpp#L5385-L5485) · [CoreCLR wait object](https://github.com/dotnet/runtime/blob/474ed963699fc29809015392738a15231aaae2de/src/coreclr/gc/gcee.cpp#L263-L308)

All 20 comparison associations overlap another mapped four-phase envelope. Ten overlap unresolved GC preparation, five unresolved collection links and five AppDomain shutdown. Looking at all 30 selected callsites, none is disjoint from every captured four-phase envelope. The comparison cannot supply a same-path control free of observed suspension. The differences above do not isolate the effect of GC on Emit variance or quantify scheduler delay. Windows and WSL produced byte-identical output and passed 22 focused tests each.
